Section 01

Competitor Landscape

Key Finding

The workshop/webinar funnel format is no longer a differentiator — at least 5 advertisers are running the same "free online Medicare workshop" model on Meta right now. The differentiation has to live in who Emily is, the independence story, and specific rules no one else names. And: since January 2025, Meta's Special Ads Category killed age-bracket and lookalike targeting for all Medicare advertisers. Everyone is now running broad. Creative copy naming a specific life situation now does the demographic qualification work. Hook specificity is a media strategy, not just a copywriting preference.

Reading the Landscape

Six advertisers, one shape. Pull back from the individual cards and the pattern is clear, and it points straight at where Emily should spend her creative.

Takeaway 01 · The moat
The format is commoditized. The operator is not.
Every rival sells the same free workshop and the same promise: "Medicare is confusing, we make it simple." Not one of them can add the next sentence: "and I take no commissions and sell no plans." That sentence is the entire moat. It is not a copy flourish, it is the one claim the whole competitive set is structurally barred from making.
Takeaway 02 · The pace-setter
MedicareSchool is the benchmark, not the ceiling.
Their ad library is the best free R&D Emily has. The two-paths binary, the "nobody told you" curiosity gap, the $200K number, and a live retargeting sequence are all validated by their spend. Adopt the mechanics and strip the liability: their funnel still ends in commissioned plan sales, so every frame Emily borrows gets upgraded by the independence claim they cannot copy back.
Takeaway 03 · The open field
Everyone competes on confusion. Nobody competes on the named rules.
The rivals stay generic: "plans," "deadlines," "confusing," "plain English." The specific, verifiable rules that actually cost people money are almost entirely unclaimed in the ad space: the HSA six-month excise trap, the IRMAA two-year lookback, the narrow-network doctor loss, the Social Security to Medicare timing link, the Part B late penalty. Specificity is not just better copy here. With Special Ads Category forcing everyone to run broad, a hook that names a real situation is now doing the audience qualification that targeting no longer can.
Takeaway 04 · The calendar
Independence reads loudest when the prospect is most besieged.
From October 15 to December 7, carrier and broker spend floods the feed with "$0 premium" and urgency mail. That noise is not a threat to Emily's message, it is the setup for it. The more the prospect feels pressured and sold to, the harder her "everyone here has a stake in your decision except me" lands. Weight the plan-leaving, network, and junk-mail angles into that window on purpose.
Section 02

Creative Patterns Currently Working

Pattern Context

These patterns are confirmed-active in the Ad Library as of August 2026. "Working" here means advertiser is investing in them repeatedly or with many ad variations — a reliable proxy for performance. All video-dominant. Average length: 1:00–2:00.

MedicareSchool.com · 4 ads using this frame
The "Two Paths" Binary Simplification
Takes an overwhelming system (dozens of plan types) and reduces it to a clean binary: Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage. The binary kills ambiguity, gives structure, and makes the choice feel manageable. Runs across at least 4 separate MedicareSchool ads — consistently.
"There are two main Medicare paths, and how they work can shape your experience in ways most people don't expect."
MedicareSchool.com · persistent CTA phrase
"Learn Before You Enroll" Education-First CTA
Used as the closing CTA line in every MedicareSchool ad. Frames registration as the smart, proactive move — not a sales funnel entry. Counters the "is this a pitch?" objection without naming it.
"The best thing you can do right now is learn before you enroll. Our free Medicare Essentials Workshop covers everything — every plan type, every deadline, every trap."
MedicareSchool.com · ad running since Jul 2026
The "Nobody Told You" Curiosity Gap
Headline positions the workshop as revealing suppressed information. Validates the prospect's sense that they're missing something while indicting the existing channels (agents, government) for the omission. Creates a "why haven't I heard this?" pull.
"What Nobody Tells You When You Turn 65."
MedicareSchool.com · fear-anchoring angle
Hospital Bill Shock ($200K Number)
Leads with a specific large dollar amount tied to what Medicare coverage (or the wrong plan) actually costs in a worst-case scenario. Specificity of the number is the mechanism — it's not vague "big bills" but $200,000, which the reader's brain treats as real.
"What a $200,000 Hospital Bill Actually Costs on Medicare."
MedicareSchool.com · contrast frame
The "Two Versions of 65" Contrast
Opens with a vivid before/after split: the version of turning 65 where Medicare is overwhelming, versus the version where you actually understand it. Puts the prospect in both pictures simultaneously — the negative for empathy, the positive for aspiration.
"There is a version of turning 65 where Medicare feels overwhelming... And there is a version where you actually understand what you're choosing between."
The Medicare Family · 6 ads
The Sarcasm / Pattern-Interrupt Opener
Opens with a knowing, slightly irreverent line that says "I get it" before anyone says anything. Breaks the scroll pattern used by every serious, earnest Medicare ad. Creates instant affinity with people who are already frustrated with the category.
"Of course Medicare is confusing! Since when does the government make anything simple?"
Gruening Health & Wealth · 3 variations
Age-64 Timeline Alert
Targets the specific 12-month window before eligibility. Creates urgency around a real calendar window — not vague "turning 65 soon" but "starting at 64." This specificity reads as expert and increases relevance with the ideal prospect timing.
"Gathering general Medicare information starting at age 64 allows you to break down the entire process step-by-step."
MedicareSchool.com · retargeting
Retargeting "Book the Call" Sequence
Separate ad creative targeting people who visited the site but didn't convert. Warm, direct, zero-pressure. Signals a mature funnel with retargeting infrastructure — not just TOF spend. Creates 2–3 more touchpoints before the conversion ask.
"We noticed you checked out our website MedicareSchool.com... but you didn't get a chance to book a call. We offer a completely free, zero-obligation phone consultation."
USA Medicare 101 · 10+ simultaneous ads
Heavy Headline Variation Testing
Running 10+ ads simultaneously with the same creative but 5 different headline variants in each ad unit. This is a scaled audience/placement testing approach — finding what hook resonates before committing budget. Signals sophistication in funnel architecture.
Variants within one ad unit: "America's #1 Workshop" / "Medicare is confusing. Are you ready?" / "Medicare made simple! Join today"
MedicareSchool.com · transition-specific
The Employer-to-Medicare Transition Frame
Targets the specific moment of leaving employer coverage — not generic "turning 65" but "you've worked past 65 and are now getting ready to retire." Hyper-specific situation makes the ad feel like it was written for them.
"If you've worked past 65 and are now getting ready to retire, Medicare isn't just something you 'start.' You're transitioning from coverage that's likely felt simple and familiar into a system that works very differently."

Section 04

Creative Recommendations

How to Read These

Each recommendation names the competitive pattern it steals from or the gap it fills, the hook (verbatim draft for the opening line or headline), the persona target, and the format. All drive to the free workshop. None sell the Concierge to cold traffic. Priority is rated on competitive gap size and likely response given the current landscape.

Format Guidelines (Meta 2026)

Video: Native/UGC-style talking head outperforms polished production by 35% in view completion (IAB 2025). Emily to camera is both highest-performing and most brand-congruent. Hook within 3 seconds. 80% of video watched without sound — captions are mandatory. Design sound-off-first. Target length: 45–90 sec for education hooks; 15–30 sec for retargeting. CPL benchmarks: Medicare workshop registrations averaged $18.14 in 2025 (up from $7.65 in 2024 due to creative fatigue). Refresh trigger: frequency above 3.5 within 7 days + 25% engagement drop.

Brand Voice Constraint

No "limited spots," no countdown pressure, no "$0 guaranteed" benefit language, no medical iconography. Protective urgency only: "don't finalize your Medicare decision until you've joined." Confirm DOJ-specific claims (carrier/broker names) with Emily before running.

Suggested Testing Sequence

Fifteen concepts is a slate, not a single batch. Run them in three waves so the calendar and the funnel stage do the sequencing:

Wave 1 — AEP-timely, cold (launch by mid-September): 01 Plan Left Town, 10 The Doctor You've Had 15 Years, 04 The $0 Premium Trap. These ride the enrollment window and the carrier noise while intent is highest.

Wave 2 — expertise and differentiation, cold: 02 HSA Tax Bomb, 08 IRMAA Sucker Punch, 15 Social Security + Medicare Timing, 13 The $500K Mistake, 14 The One Question. Each names a rule or a stance no rival is running, so they qualify the audience by hook alone.

Wave 3 — new doors and warm funnel: 06 The Caregiver Entry Point, 11 That "URGENT" Envelope, 12 Three Hours on Medicare.gov, 03 Two Versions of 65, 07 Sarcasm Opener, then 09 Retargeting to harvest everyone the first two waves warmed. Hold 05 The DOJ Angle until Emily and legal clear the specific claims.

Test 3 to 4 concepts per ad set, one variable at a time. Kill on frequency above 3.5 with a 25% engagement drop; scale any hook that beats the $18.14 CPL benchmark.
